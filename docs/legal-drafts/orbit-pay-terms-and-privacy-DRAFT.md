## Part A. Terms of Service: new and replacement sections

### A1. What Orbit is (new section, place after "Description of Service")

Orbit is business software for service businesses. Orbit is not a bank, a
mobile money operator, a payment service provider, or a deposit-taking
institution. Orbit does not hold, store or keep money for you or for your
clients, and Orbit does not offer accounts, balances, deposits or withdrawals.

[How it works: since October 2026 every Orbit Pay payment is split by Paystack

### A2. Orbit Pay (new section)

**2.1 What Orbit Pay is.** Orbit Pay lets you send your clients a link to pay
deposits and invoices by card or bank transfer. Payments are processed by
Paystack Payments Limited ("Paystack"), a licensed payment processor.

**2.2 Paystack's terms apply.** When you use Orbit Pay you also agree to
Paystack's terms of service and acceptable use policy, which govern how
payments are processed and settled. Your clients agree to Paystack's terms
when they pay. If Paystack's terms and these Terms conflict on how a payment
is processed or settled, Paystack's terms apply to that payment.

**2.3 Who can use it.** Orbit Pay is currently available only to businesses
based in Nigeria, invoicing in naira, with a Nigerian bank account in the name
of the business or its owner. We may add other countries later.

**2.4 Setting up.** To use Orbit Pay you give us the bank and account number
you want to be paid into. We check the account name with Paystack and ask you
to confirm it. You authorise Orbit to create and manage a Paystack
"subaccount" for your business using these details, and to share your
business name, bank details and contact email with Paystack for that purpose.
You must keep these details accurate. Paystack may ask you for more
information to verify your identity or business.

**2.5 Where your money goes.** Payments made through Orbit Pay are settled by
Paystack directly into the bank account you provided, on Paystack's normal
settlement schedule. Orbit never receives, holds or controls your share of a
payment. Orbit cannot speed up, delay or redirect a settlement. Questions
about a specific settlement may need to be raised with Paystack.

**2.6 Orbit Pay fees.** Orbit charges a fee on each successful Orbit Pay
payment, calculated on the amount you are charging for your service:

| Plan | Nigerian cards, bank transfer | Cards issued outside Nigeria (if enabled) |
|---|---|---|
| Free | 2.5% + ₦100, capped at ₦3,500 | 5% + ₦150, no cap |
| Pro | 2% + ₦100, capped at ₦2,500 | 4.5% + ₦150, no cap |

Fees are rounded up to the nearest naira. The fee is fixed when you create the
payment link, at the plan you are on at that moment. Orbit pays Paystack's
processing charges out of its own fee; you are not charged them separately.

**2.7 Who pays the fee.** The Orbit Pay fee is paid by your client. Your client
pays the invoice amount plus the fee, shown to them as a separate "Processing
fee" line before they pay, and you receive the full invoice amount.

**2.8 Fee changes.** We may change Orbit Pay fees with at least [30] days'
notice by email and in the app. New fees apply only to payment links created
after the change.

**2.9 Refunds.** You are responsible for refunds to your clients. You can
start a refund from the paid invoice in Orbit. Refunds are processed by
Paystack and are deducted from your future settlements. If your future
settlements are not enough, Paystack may recover the amount from you in line
with its terms. [Lawyer: confirm with Paystack whether Orbit's fee on a
refunded split payment is reversed. Options: (a) Orbit's fee is not refunded;
(b) Orbit's fee is refunded on full refunds made within X days. Pick one and
reflect it here.]

**2.10 Chargebacks and disputes.** If a client's bank disputes a payment
(a "chargeback"), Paystack handles the dispute with you directly. You agree to
respond to Paystack's requests for evidence in time. If a dispute is decided
against you, the disputed amount, and any dispute fee Paystack charges, are
recovered from your settlements by Paystack. Orbit will notify you of
disputes we are told about but is not a party to them.

**2.11 Your responsibilities.** You must only use Orbit Pay to take payment
for lawful goods and services that you actually provide, in line with
Paystack's acceptable use policy. You are responsible for your prices, your
receipts, your relationship with your clients, and your own taxes (including
VAT and income tax) on money you receive.

**2.12 Suspension.** We may pause or end your access to Orbit Pay if Paystack
asks us to, if we reasonably suspect fraud or misuse, or if required by law.
Pausing Orbit Pay does not affect payments already settled to you. You can
always record payments you receive outside Orbit (bank transfer, cash) at no
fee.

### A3. Payment Terms (replacement for the existing "Payment Terms" section)

**3.1 Subscriptions.** Orbit Free costs nothing and allows up to 10 clients.
Orbit Pro is billed in advance, monthly or yearly.

**3.2 Price and currency.** In Nigeria, Pro is ₦7,500 per month or ₦75,000 per
year. In other countries we show an equivalent price in your local currency
for convenience, but subscriptions are charged in Nigerian naira; your bank
converts the charge and may apply its own exchange rate and fees.

**3.3 Free trial.** New Pro subscriptions may start with a [7]-day free trial.
A card is required. Unless you cancel before the trial ends, your subscription
starts and you are charged at the end of the trial.

**3.4 Renewal and cancellation.** Subscriptions renew automatically until
cancelled. You can cancel anytime in Settings; you keep Pro until the end of
the period you paid for.

**3.5 Refunds of subscription fees.** Subscription fees are non-refundable
except where required by law.

**3.6 Price changes.** We may change subscription prices with at least [30]
days' notice. A change applies from your next renewal after the notice.

**3.7 Moving from Pro to Free.** If you stop paying for Pro, your data is kept
and remains visible. You will not be able to add clients beyond the Free
limit until you upgrade again.

**3.8 Payment processing.** Subscription payments are processed by Paystack.
Orbit does not see or store your full card details.

## Part B. Privacy Policy: new and replacement sections

### B1. Payment information we collect (add to "Information We Collect")

**From businesses using Orbit Pay:** your settlement bank's name, the last
four digits of your account number, the account name returned by your bank,
and the Paystack subaccount reference for your business. Your full account
number is sent to Paystack to set up your payouts and is not kept by Orbit.

**About each Orbit Pay payment:** the amount, Orbit's fee, the processing fee
setting, the payment status, Paystack's payment reference, the date and time,
the payment method type (for example card or bank transfer), the country that
issued the card, Paystack's processing charge, and any refund or dispute
status.

**We never collect or store** card numbers, card expiry dates, CVV/CVC codes,
card PINs, one-time passwords, or online banking passwords. These go directly
to Paystack.

**From your clients when they pay:** their email address (or a placeholder if
you did not record one) is passed to Paystack so Paystack can send a receipt.

### B2. Who we share payment information with (add to "How We Share Your Information")

**Paystack**, to process payments and settle money to your bank account:
your business name, contact email, bank details, and payment amounts; your
client's email address for receipts. Paystack processes this under its own
privacy policy.

### B3. Our role for your clients' data (new section)

When you add your clients to Orbit, you decide what information to record and
why. For that client information, you are the data controller and Orbit acts
as your data processor, handling it only to provide Orbit to you. You are
responsible for having a lawful basis to record and share your clients'
information, including for Orbit Pay receipts.

### B4. Service providers (replacement list)

We use these providers to run Orbit. They process data only on our
instructions:

- Supabase: database and sign-in
- Vercel: hosting
- Paystack: subscriptions and Orbit Pay payments
- Resend: sending emails
- PostHog: product analytics (which features are used; we do not send client
  names, messages, or payment details)
- Groq, Google (Gemini) and Pollinations: generating Orbit AI suggestions when
  you use AI features (we send only what the feature needs)
- Google, Apple and Mozilla push services: delivering phone notifications you
  turn on

### B5. How long we keep payment records (add to "Data Retention")

We keep records of Orbit Pay payments, fees and refunds for [six] years after
the payment, to meet tax, accounting and anti-fraud obligations, even if you
delete your account. Other account data is deleted or anonymised within [90]
days of account deletion.

### B6. Your rights (amend "Your Rights Under the GDPR")

Retitle to "Your rights" and cover the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 as
well as the GDPR where it applies: access, correction, deletion, objection,
restriction, data portability, and the right to complain to the Nigeria Data
Protection Commission (or your local regulator).

## Part C. Questions for the lawyer

1. Does structuring Orbit Pay as Paystack split payments (subaccounts), with
   no Orbit-controlled balance, keep Orbit outside CBN licensing for payment
   service providers and mobile money operators? Is any registration,
   agreement or disclosure still required (e.g. a "technical service provider"
   or "aggregator" category)?
2. Does Orbit need a separate written agreement with Paystack for operating
   subaccounts on behalf of other businesses (platform or marketplace terms)?
3. Refunds: should Orbit's fee be returned on refunded payments (see 2.9)?
4. Is the merchant-as-controller / Orbit-as-processor split (B3) correct under
   the NDPA 2023, and does Orbit need to register with the NDPC or file a
   compliance audit?
5. VAT: is VAT due on Orbit's Orbit Pay fees and subscriptions, and must prices
   be shown VAT-inclusive?
6. Showing a local-currency price while charging in naira (3.2): is the
   disclosure sufficient under consumer protection rules in Ghana, Kenya,
   South Africa and the CFA countries?
7. Record retention period (B5): is six years correct for these records?
8. The terms currently provide for arbitration in Nigeria; is that suitable
   for businesses outside Nigeria?
9. The client always pays the "Processing fee" on top of the invoice. Is that
   surcharge allowed, and is any wording or disclosure needed under consumer
   protection or card scheme surcharging rules?
